Overview

Mercury is the smallest planet in our solar system and the closest to the Sun. Despite being the nearest to our star, it's not the hottest planet - that title goes to Venus. Mercury is a world of extremes with scorching days and freezing nights.

Planet TypeTerrestrial (Rocky)
Position1st from the Sun (innermost planet)
DiscoveryKnown since ancient times
Named AfterRoman messenger god (swift movement)
📏

Dimensions & Size

Mercury is only slightly larger than Earth's Moon and is the smallest planet in our solar system. Despite its small size, Mercury is dense and compact, making it the second-densest planet after Earth.

Diameter4,879 km (3,032 mi)
Equatorial Circumference15,329 km
Volume60,827,208,742 km³
Earth Comparison0.38x Earth diameter
Size RankSmallest planet (since Pluto's reclassification)
⚖

Mass & Gravity

Mercury has the second-highest density of any planet due to its large iron core, which makes up about 75% of the planet's radius. On Mercury, you would weigh only 38% of your Earth weight.

Mass3.285 x 10²³ kg
Earth Mass Comparison0.055x Earth's mass (5.5%)
Density 5.427 g/cm³ (2nd highest after Earth!)
Surface Gravity3.7 m/s² (0.38× Earth)
⛅

Atmosphere (Exosphere)

Mercury has essentially no atmosphere, only an exosphere made of atoms blasted off its surface by solar wind and micrometeorite impacts. This means no weather, no clouds, and no protection from space debris.

CompositionOxygen, Sodium, Hydrogen, Helium, Potassium
TypeExosphere (extremely thin)
Pressure~10⁻¹⁵ bar (virtually zero)
SourceSolar wind and radioactive decay
🌪

Major Discoveries

Despite its proximity to the Sun, Mercury has water ice in permanently shadowed craters at its poles! Other discoveries include mysterious "hollows" (bright depressions), evidence that the planet has shrunk, and a weak but active magnetic field.

Water IceFound in permanently shadowed polar craters
Magnetic FieldUnexpected discovery by Mariner 10
HollowsMysterious bright depressions on surface
Volcanic PastAncient volcanic plains discovered
🌡

Extreme Temperature

Mercury experiences the most extreme temperature swings in the solar system - a whopping 610°C difference between day and night! Without an atmosphere to trap heat, temperatures plummet rapidly after sunset.

Daytime Temperature430°C (800°F)
Nighttime Temperature-180°C (-290°F)
Temperature Range610°C variation (largest in solar system!)
Polar CratersWater ice in permanently shadowed regions
🌐

Surface & Geology

Mercury's surface is heavily cratered like the Moon, preserving a 4-billion-year record of impacts. The planet has shrunk over time as its core cooled, creating enormous cliffs called scarps that run for hundreds of kilometers.

Surface TypeCratered, similar to the Moon
Largest CraterCaloris Basin - 1,550 km diameter
CompositionSilicate rock and metal
Volcanic ActivityAncient lava plains
🪃

Orbit & Speed

Mercury zips around the Sun faster than any other planet at 47.87 km/s (107,000 mph)! Its orbit is also the most elliptical, taking it from 46 to 70 million km from the Sun. This helped prove Einstein's theory of relativity.

Distance from Sun 57.9 million km (0.39 AU)
Orbital Speed47.87 km/s (fastest planet!)
Orbital Period87.97 Earth days
Orbital Eccentricity0.206 (most elliptical orbit)
Perihelion/Aphelion46-70 million km from Sun
🌙

Moons & Satellites

Mercury has no moons or natural satellites. Its proximity to the Sun and high orbital speed make it extremely difficult for Mercury to capture and hold onto a moon - the Sun's gravity would pull it away.

Total Moons0 (no natural satellites)
Why No Moon?Too close to Sun - Sun's gravity dominates
Capture DifficultyHigh orbital speed prevents moon capture
Unique AmongOnly Venus also has no moons (inner planets)
🚀

Space Exploration

Mercury is one of the least-explored planets due to the difficulty of reaching it. Getting into Mercury orbit requires slowing down tremendously against the Sun's gravity. BepiColombo will be only the third mission to visit.

First FlybyMariner 10 (1974-75) - 3 Flybys
First OrbitMESSENGER (2011-2015)
Current MissionBepiColombo (ESA/JAXA, arriving 2025)
DifficultyHard to reach - requires precise gravity assists
