Oort Cloud

The Oort Cloud is a vast spherical shell of icy bodies surrounding the Solar System, marking the distant boundary of the Sun’s gravitational influence.

What is the Oort Cloud?

The Oort Cloud is believed to contain trillions of icy objects — remnants from the early Solar System that were pushed outward by gravitational interactions with the giant planets.

It represents the outermost structure of the Solar System, extending almost halfway to the nearest star.

Distance

Begins near 2,000 AU and extends up to 100,000 AU — nearly 1.5 light-years from the Sun.

Shape

A massive spherical cloud surrounding the Solar System in all directions.

Comet Origin

Long-period comets originate from here, disturbed by passing stars or galactic tides.

Composition

Icy bodies made of frozen methane, ammonia, water ice, and dust.

Discovery

Proposed by Dutch astronomer Jan Oort in 1950 to explain long-period comet behavior.

Visibility

Too distant, dark, and small to be directly observed with present-day technology.

Solar Boundary

Its outer edge marks the true end of the Solar System.

Nearby Stars

Passing stars like Proxima Centauri can influence Oort Cloud objects.
