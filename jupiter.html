Overview

Jupiter is the largest planet in our solar system, a giant world of swirling clouds and storms. It has a mass more than twice that of all the other planets combined.

Planet TypeGas Giant
Position5th from the Sun
DiscoveryKnown since ancient times
Named AfterRoman king of the gods
📏

Dimensions & Size

Jupiter is so massive that it could swallow more than 1,300 Earths. Its equator is about 11 times wider than Earth's.

Diameter142,984 km (88,846 mi)
Equatorial Circumference439,264 km
Volume1,431,281,810,739 km3
Earth Comparison11 Earths across diameter
Volume Comparison1,321 Earths could fit inside
⚖

Mass & Gravity

Jupiter contains more than twice the mass of all other planets combined. If you weigh 100 pounds on Earth, you would weigh 253 pounds on Jupiter.

Mass 1.898 x 102 kg
Earth Mass Comparison317.8 x Earth's mass
Density 1.326 g/cm3
Surface Gravity24.79 m/s2
⛅

Atmosphere & Weather

Jupiter's atmosphere is a chaotic laboratory of extreme weather, with powerful storms, lightning, and auroras. The planet has no solid surface beneath its thick atmosphere.

Composition90% Hydrogen, 10% Helium
Trace ElementsMethane, Ammonia, Water vapor
Cloud LayersAmmonia, Ammonium Hydrosulfide, Water
Wind SpeedsUp to 640 km/h
🌪

The Great Red Spot

The Great Red Spot is a gigantic storm that has been raging for centuries. It's large enough to swallow Earth whole and rotates counterclockwise.

TypeAnticyclonic storm
Size16,350 km wide (larger than Earth)
AgeAt least 400 years old
Wind SpeedsUp to 432 km/h
🌡

Temperature

Jupiter radiates more energy than it receives from the Sun. The internal heat is generated by gravitational compression of the planet.

Cloud Top Temperature-145°C (-234°F)
Core Temperature~24,000°C (43,000°F)
Average Temperature-110°C (-166°F)
Heat RadiationEmits more heat than it receives
🌐

Surface & Interior

Jupiter doesn't have a solid surface to stand on. Deep below the clouds, hydrogen gas transforms into liquid and then metallic hydrogen under extreme pressure.

Surface TypeNo solid surface
Interior LayersGaseous, Liquid, Metallic hydrogen
CoreRocky/icy core (10-15 Earth masses)
Magnetic FieldStrongest in solar system
🪃

Rotation & Orbit

Jupiter rotates faster than any other planet in the solar system. A day on Jupiter lasts less than 10 hours, causing the planet to bulge at its equator.

Day Length9.9 hours (fastest rotation)
Year Length11.86 Earth years
Distance from Sun778.5 million km (5.2 AU)
Orbital Speed13.07 km/s
Axial Tilt3.13° (nearly upright)
🌙

Moons & Rings

Jupiter has the most moons in our solar system. The four largest (Galilean moons) were discovered by Galileo in 1610 and are fascinating worlds themselves.

Total Moons95 confirmed moons
Galilean MoonsIo, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto
Largest MoonGanymede (5,268 km diameter)
Ring SystemFaint rings discovered in 1979
Ring CompositionDust particles from moons
🚀

Space Exploration

Multiple spacecraft have visited Jupiter, providing invaluable data about this giant planet. The Juno mission is currently orbiting Jupiter, studying its composition and magnetic field.

First FlybyPioneer 10 (1973)
First OrbiterGalileo (1995-2003)
Current MissionJuno (arrived 2016)
Notable MissionsVoyager 1 & 2, Cassini, New Horizons
Future MissionEuropa Clipper (launching 2024)
