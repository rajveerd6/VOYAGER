History & Formation of the Solar System

The Big Bang ( 13.8 Billion Years Ago )

Everything began with the Big Bang — an enormous expansion that created space, time, matter, and energy. The universe was a hot, dense fireball filled with plasma. As it expanded, it cooled, allowing the formation of the first atoms — hydrogen and helium.

Birth of the Solar Nebula (4.6 Billion Years Ago)

After billions of years, giant stars lived and died, creating heavier elements. A massive nearby supernova shock wave compressed a giant cloud of gas and dust — the Solar Nebula. Gravity pulled material inward, forming a spinning disk.

Formation of the Sun

At the center of the collapsing nebula, pressure and temperature increased until nuclear fusion ignited. Hydrogen began turning into helium, and the Sun was born. Its heat pushed lighter material outward, while gravity pulled heavier, rocky and metallic materials toward the inner regions. The material that remained in orbit formed a flattened, spinning protoplanetary disk, often compared to a pancake, around the young star.

Formation of Planets

Dust and rocks collided, forming planetesimals — the building blocks of planets.
Tiny dust grains in a protoplanetary disk stick together through gentle collisions, growing into pebble-sized clumps, then kilometer-sized planetesimals (the building blocks), which further aggregate via gravity and impacts to form protoplanets and eventually f ull-fledged planets like Earth, with leftover bits becoming asteroids and comets.

Early Earth was a molten world, constantly bombarded by asteroids. Slowly, its surface cooled.

Inner rocky worlds stabilized while outer planets collected gas to become giants.

The Solar System as We Know It

After millions of years, the Sun settled, planets formed stable orbits, moons appeared, and leftover comets filled the Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud. The Solar System became the structured space neighborhood we explore today.
