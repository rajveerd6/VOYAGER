Overview

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and Earth's outer neighbor. Often called the Red Planet due to iron oxide on its surface, Mars has captivated humanity for millennia and is the primary target for human exploration.

Planet TypeTerrestrial (Rocky)
Position4th from the Sun
DiscoveryKnown since ancient times
Named AfterRoman god of war
📏

Dimensions & Size

Mars is about half the size of Earth. Despite being smaller, it has some of the most spectacular geological features in the solar system, including the largest volcano and canyon.

Diameter6,792 km (4,220 mi)
Equatorial Circumference21,344 km
Volume163,115,609,799 km3
Earth Comparison0.53x Earths across diameter
Volume Comparison0.15x Earth Volume
⚖

Mass & Gravity

Mars has about 38% of Earth's gravity. If you weigh 100 pounds on Earth, you would weigh only 38 pounds on Mars. This lower gravity would make it easier to move around but poses challenges for long-term human health.

Mass 6.417 x 1023 kg
Earth Mass Comparison0.107x Earth's mass (about 10%)
Density 3.93 g/cm3
Surface Gravity3.71 m/s2

Olympus Mons

Olympus Mons is the tallest volcano and mountain in the solar system. It's so large that if you stood on its summit, its base would extend beyond the horizon. The volcano may still be active.

TypeShield volcano (largest in solar system)
Height21.9 km (13.6 mi) - 2.5x Mt. Everest!
Base Diametere600 km (similar to Arizona)
Caldera80 km wide, 3 km deep

Temperature

Mars is a cold desert world with extreme temperature variations. The thin atmosphere means temperatures can change dramatically between day and night. Mars has seasons similar to Earth due to its tilted axis.

Summer High20°C (68°F) at equator
Winter Low-125°C (-193°F) at poles
Average Temperature-63°C (-81°F)
SeasonsHas seasons like Earth (axial tilt)
🌐

Surface & Geology

Mars's surface is a rust-colored desert covered with rocks, dust, and dramatic geological features. Evidence suggests Mars once had liquid water on its surface billions of years ago.

Surface TypeRocky with iron oxide (rust)
ColourRed/orange from iron oxide
Major FeaturesVolcanoes, canyons, craters, ice caps
Polar Ice CapsWater ice and dry ice (CO₂)
🪃

Rotation & Orbit

A day on Mars (called a "sol") is remarkably similar to Earth's day. Mars also has a similar axial tilt to Earth, giving it four distinct seasons, though they last nearly twice as long.

Day Length24.6 hours (similar to Earth!)
Year Length687 Earth days (1.88 Earth years)
Distance from Sun228 million km (1.52 AU)
Orbital Speed24.07 km/s
Axial Tilt25.19° (very similar to Earth)
🌙

Moons & Satellites

Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons named after the sons of the Roman god Mars: Phobos (fear) and Deimos (panic). They're likely captured asteroids and are much smaller than Earth's Moon

Total Moons2 small moons
Moon NamePhobos and Deimos
First Successful FlybyMariner 4 (1965)
OrbitersMultiple active spacecraft
Active RoversCuriosity (2012), Perseverance (2021)

Water on Mars

Mars once had abundant liquid water on its surface. Today, water exists as ice at the poles and underground. Scientists have found evidence of seasonal water flows, raising hopes for potential life.

Current WaterIce at poles and underground
Past WaterEvidence of ancient rivers, lakes, ocean
Subsurface IceDetected by radar and landers
Seasonal FlowsRecurring slope lineae (possible brine)
Polar IcePermanent water ice caps
