Overview

Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun. It was the first planet located through mathematical predictions rather than direct observation, making it a triumph of mathematics and astronomy.

Planet TypeIce Giant
Position8th from the Sun (outermost planet)
DiscoverySeptember 23, 1846
Named AfterRoman god of the sea
📏

Dimensions & Size

Neptune is the fourth-largest planet in our solar system and slightly smaller than Uranus. Despite being smaller, Neptune is more massive than Uranus due to its higher density.

Diameter49,528 km (30,775 mi)
Equatorial Circumference155,600 km
Volume62,525,703,987,421 km3
Earth Comparison3.9 Earths across diameter
Volume Comparison57 Earths could fit inside
⚖

Mass & Gravity

Neptune is more massive than Uranus despite being slightly smaller. Its gravity is about 14% stronger than Earth's. If you weigh 100 pounds on Earth, you would weigh 114 pounds on Neptune.

Mass 1.024 x 1026 kg
Earth Mass Comparison17.1 x Earth's mass
Density 1.638 g/cm3
Surface Gravity11.15 m/s2
⛅

Atmosphere & Weather

Neptune has the fastest winds in the solar system, reaching supersonic speeds of up to 2,100 km/h. Its vivid blue color comes from methane in its atmosphere absorbing red light.

Composition80% Hydrogen, 19% Helium, 1% Methane
Trace ElementsWater, Ammonia, Hydrogen Deuteride
Cloud LayersMethane, Ammonia, Water ice clouds
Wind SpeedsUp to 2,100 km/h (fastest in solar system!)
🌪

The Great Dark Spot

The Great Dark Spot was a massive storm system discovered by Voyager 2 in 1989. Similar to Jupiter's Great Red Spot, it was large enough to contain Earth, but it disappeared within a few years.

TypeAnticyclonic storm system
Size13,000 × 6,600 km (Earth-sized)
Current StatusDisappeared by 1994
DiscoveryVoyager 2 (1989)
🌡

Temperature

Despite being the farthest planet from the Sun, Neptune is warmer than Uranus. It radiates more than twice the energy it receives from the Sun, with internal heat driving its extreme weather.

Cloud Top Temperature-214°C (-353°F)
Core Temperature~5,100°C (9,200°F)
Average Temperature-200°C (-328°F)
Heat Radiation2.6x more than it receives from Sun
🌐

Surface & Interior

Neptune is an ice giant with a mantle of water, methane, and ammonia ices surrounding a rocky core. Like Uranus, its magnetic field is strangely tilted and doesn't pass through the planet's center.

Surface TypeNo solid surface
Interior LayersWater, methane, ammonia ices
CoreRocky/icy core (1.2 Earth masses)
Magnetic FieldTilted 47° from rotation axis
🪃

Rotation & Orbit

Neptune takes 165 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun. Since its discovery in 1846, it has completed only one full orbit in 2011. A day on Neptune lasts just over 16 hours.

Day Length16.1 hours
Year Length164.8 Earth years
Distance from Sun4.5 billion km (30.1 AU)
Orbital Speed5.43 km/s
Axial Tilt28.32° (similar to Earth)
🌙

Moons & Rings

Neptune has 16 known moons. Triton is the largest and most fascinating, orbiting backwards and shooting nitrogen geysers into space. Scientists believe Triton was captured from the Kuiper Belt. Neptune has 5 main rings and several fainter dust bands.

Total Moons16 confirmed moons
Major MoonsTriton, Proteus, Nereid, Larissa
Largest MoonTriton (2,707 km diameter)
Ring System5 main rings + additional arcs
Ring CompositionDust particles, possibly organic material
🚀

Space Exploration

Only Voyager 2 has visited Neptune, in 1989. It discovered 6 moons, confirmed the ring system, and revealed the Great Dark Spot. NASA is considering the Neptune Odyssey mission for future exploration.

MissionsOnly Voyager 2 (1989)
Flyby DateAugust 25, 1989
Closest Approach4,950 km from cloud tops
Discoveries6 new moons, rings, Great Dark Spot
Future MissionNeptune Odyssey proposed for 2030s
